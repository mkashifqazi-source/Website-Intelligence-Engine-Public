# Research and acceptance workflow

1. Establish that the research environment actually traverses evidence; reject ungrounded runs.
2. Research current primary documentation and standards across the required website-signal domains.
3. Construct atomic candidates with source provenance and scoped interpretation.
4. Normalize language/identity, detect equivalent concepts and merge only genuine duplicates.
5. Traverse lifecycle, entity, relationship, failure and temporal dimensions to find missing coverage.
6. Assess saturation and structured quality control.
7. Produce the canonical machine-readable corpus with resolvable references.
8. Independently audit and formally freeze the accepted universe.
9. Only then consider a separate next-phase gate.

Semantic atomicity means one independently interpretable property, not an arbitrary multiplication of percentiles, units or time windows. Corpus size emerges from defensible evidence, rather than a target count.

The status vocabulary is important. A successful research-environment smoke test permits evidence collection but does not constitute Phase-1 acceptance. A generated report is not a canonical corpus, a structurally valid corpus is not audited evidence, and an independent audit does not silently authorize implementation without the formal freeze/gate.

Freshness-sensitive platform claims require current evidence. Retrieval date, publication/update date and status-as-of date are different observations. Unknown dates remain unknown. Cross-source conflict must preserve its scope and provenance rather than be resolved by averaging incompatible claims.
